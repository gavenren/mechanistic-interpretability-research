# Source record

Checked: 13 October 2026. Primary source pages and the close full-text methods were checked. This is a targeted literature gate, not a proof that no earlier study exists. The basic dependence of neuron-level interpretation on the coordinate basis is established work. The change-of-coordinates identity is elementary algebra.

1. David Bau, Bolei Zhou, Aditya Khosla, Aude Oliva, and Antonio Torralba. 2017. *Network Dissection: Quantifying Interpretability of Deep Visual Representations*. CVPR. [Official paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Bau_Network_Dissection_Quantifying_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf). Section 3.2 rotates hidden representations and compensates the downstream map. It directly overlaps with a broad claim that unchanged behavior can coexist with changed unit interpretability.
2. Atticus Geiger, Zhengxuan Wu, Christopher Potts, Thomas Icard, and Noah Goodman. 2024. *Finding Alignments Between Interpretable Causal Variables and Distributed Neural Representations*. CLeaR, PMLR 236:160–187. [Publisher page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/geiger24a.html). Distributed alignment search already uses non-standard bases and subspace interchange interventions. Our known transported operator is an oracle control, not a competing discovery algorithm.
3. Charles Godfrey, Davis Brown, Tegan Emerson, and Henry Kvinge. 2022; arXiv v5 revised 2023. *On the Symmetries of Deep Learning Models and their Internal Representations*. [Version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14258). It relates architecture and activation functions to the symmetry groups of internal representations. Our coordinate change is at a linear interface. It is not a rotation through a tanh nonlinearity.
4. Nelson Elhage et al. 2022. *Toy Models of Superposition*. [Paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10652). It studies features represented in fewer dimensions than the number of features. Our invertible eight-dimensional coordinate maps do not test compression or overcomplete feature packing.
5. Aleksandar Makelov, Georg Lange, and Neel Nanda. 2023, arXiv v2. *Is This the Subspace You Are Looking for? An Interpretability Illusion for Subspace Activation Patching*. [Paper record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030). Successful subspace patches can reflect an intervention-induced pathway rather than the mechanism used intact. Exact transport only establishes that two coordinate descriptions implement the same intervention.
6. Chuqin Geng, Li Zhang, Haolin Ye, Mark Zhang, Luke Zhang, and Xujie Si. 2026. *Are We Recovering Mechanisms? Objective-Level Recovery Gaps in Mechanistic Interpretability*. arXiv v1, 1 October. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02098v1). This work addresses objective-level mechanism recovery gaps, including candidate comparisons at equal size. Equal rank here does not give equal candidate-family coverage or prove mechanism recovery.

The specific contribution is a reproducible controlled audit on a new finite task: separate candidate-family changes from operator-transport errors, with signed-permutation controls and non-orthogonal transformations. The architecture supplies the source factorization. Neither the rotation issue nor the transport identity is claimed as a new scientific principle.
