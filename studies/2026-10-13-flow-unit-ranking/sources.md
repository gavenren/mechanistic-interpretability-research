# Source record

Checked on 13 October 2026. These are primary sources. This search limits the claim of novelty. It does not establish priority. The paper is a small empirical selection benchmark.

1. **Dinh, Sohl-Dickstein, and Bengio. Density estimation using Real NVP. ICLR 2017.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08803), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08803). We read the method in Sections 3.1–3.5 and Equations 2–12. The affine coupling, exact inverse, triangular Jacobian, alternating partitions, and likelihood training are established methods. Our model uses these methods. It is not a new flow architecture.

2. **Theis, van den Oord, and Bethge. A note on the evaluation of generative models. ICLR 2016.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01844), [author page](https://theis.io/publications/17/). We read the abstract and author summary. They show that likelihood and other sample evaluation criteria can disagree. We do not claim to discover a general conflict between likelihood and output quality. Our measure is quadrant-frequency change after a fixed internal edit, not visual sample quality.

3. **Bau and colleagues. Understanding the Role of Individual Units in a Deep Neural Network. PNAS 2020.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.05041), [author project](https://dissect.csail.mit.edu/), [journal text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7720226/). We read the abstract and author method summary. Internal-unit removal and activation in image generators already have causal uses. This study does not introduce generator unit intervention or infer that its two-dimensional units have a human semantic meaning.

4. **Malnick, Avidan, and Fried. Taming Normalizing Flows. WACV 2024.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16488), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16488), [author publication list](https://www.malnick.net/publications/). The inspected arXiv full text is version 2, dated 3 April 2023. We read Sections 3.1–3.3, Algorithm 1, Sections 4.2, 5 and 5.1, and Appendices A and B. This is close prior work. It already uses a two-dimensional Real NVP example with five Gaussian components. It changes weights to remove a target component while retaining other data. Its evaluation includes likelihood, generated attribute frequency, and fixed latent inputs before and after edits. Its method uses forgetting and remembering losses; its ablation study removes loss terms. We found no all-hidden-unit singleton ranking and held-out selector test in those inspected sections. This absence in a focused read is not proof of novelty.

The present study holds trained weights fixed. It removes every specified single hidden unit in both flow directions. It selects a unit using calibration likelihood damage or calibration quadrant change, then compares those two selections on independent fixed test samples. The exact model, samples, split, controls, and endpoint are fixed before the study runs. The contribution is this bounded measurement, not the general evaluation distinction or the flow-editing method.

The local derivative argument in the plan follows the established normalization identity: the integral of the derivative of a normalized density is zero, when the derivative can pass under the integral. It is a motivation only. It gives no guarantee for finite unit removal or an imperfectly fitted density.

5. **Vadakkeeveetil Sreelatha and colleagues. RAIGen: Rare Attribute Identification in Text-to-Image Generative Models. arXiv 2602.06806v2, 30 May 2026.** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06806v2). We inspected the abstract and the method overview. RAIGen ranks sparse-autoencoder features from diffusion models by rarity and semantic distinction. It evaluates attribute presence and amplification. This is nearby work on generator feature selection. It does not establish that our selector comparison is new. Our fixed experiment concerns exact flow likelihood damage and quadrant-frequency change after the same singleton removal.
