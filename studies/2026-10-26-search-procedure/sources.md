# Source check

Checked on 26 October 2026. These sources establish relevant prior work. They do not establish a priority claim for this study.

- Arthur Conmy and colleagues, [Towards Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997v4), 2023. The paper automates circuit discovery through patching and discusses algorithm choices. Our complete eight-component tables test a small conditional pruning problem. They do not replace its transformer evidence or establish a new pruning principle.
- Fred Zhang and Neel Nanda, [Towards Best Practices of Activation Patching in Language Models: Metrics and Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042v2), 2024. The paper shows why score and method choices matter. Our fixed-score comparison extends the repository's diagnostic tests to the saved selector models.
- Stefan Heimersheim and Neel Nanda, [How to use and interpret activation patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255v1), 2024. Its interpretation limits apply to selected edits and circuits. A conditional minimum within eight coordinates is not a complete explanation.
- The official [statsmodels proportion interval documentation](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportion_confint.html) distinguishes Wilson intervals, sidedness, and approximate coverage. The code implements the declared two-sided formula directly. It does not depend on statsmodels or claim exact finite-sample coverage.
