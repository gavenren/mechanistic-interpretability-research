## Method and claim checks

The ordinary model code preserves Study 1's initialization, full-batch loss, gradients, Adam arithmetic, parameter order, learning rate, and 4,000-update schedule. It uses eight fixed fresh seeds. It completes all eight models before the ability decision. A failed ordinary panel remains in the record and blocks the edit phases.

The intervention split keeps each receiver/request block in one set. Learning, selection, and final intervention pairs are separate. The ordinary task states are known during training. The plan states this limit.

The edit comparison uses all 220 three-neuron masks and four fixed rotation checkpoints. The learned edit has exactly 4,000 updates per model. Its three learning groups have equal weight. The final choices use selection data only. No eligible mask or checkpoint is an explicit result, not a reason to change a limit or train longer.

The final rule checks active different-value accuracy separately from all different-value accuracy. It also checks both target probability distances and same-value answer changes. The improvement claim requires every absolute gate, a mean active-case gain of at least five percentage points, and a positive gain in at least six models. Missing eligibility prevents that claim. These are practical criteria on a small finite task. They do not use the normal test that failed Experiment 2B, and they are not a population significance test.

The output checks use separate affine arithmetic. The rotation replay uses rotate, replace, and undo, while the primary calculation uses a rank-three projection. A separate matrix-exponential calculation checks each saved rotation. Saved outputs are reopened and checked. Shared data and helper functions remain, so this is not a fully independent reproduction of the entire experiment.
